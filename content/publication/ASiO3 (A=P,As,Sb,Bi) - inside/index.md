abstract: >-
  Silicon-based perovskites have emerged as promising materials for
  applications in electronics, photovoltaics, optoelectronic systems,
  and high-temperature device technologies. In this work, a
  first-principles investigation is performed on silicon-based cubic
  ASiO3 (A = P, As, Sb, Bi) perovskites. The optimized lattice
  constants are calculated to be 3.57 Å for PSiO3, 3.61 Å for
  AsSiO3, 3.74 Å for SbSiO3, and 3.78 Å for BiSiO3, confirming
  their cubic structure within the GGA-PBE approximation. The
  Goldschmidt tolerance factors, together with the Born stability
  criteria, confirm the structural and mechanical stability of the
  studied compounds. The calculated bulk, shear, and Young's moduli
  indicate strong mechanical robustness, while Pugh's and Poisson's
  ratios reveal ductile behavior for PSiO3, AsSiO3, and BiSiO3
  and brittle behavior for SbSiO3. ELATE-3D analysis further
  demonstrates anisotropic mechanical characteristics. Among the
  studied materials, PSiO3 exhibits favorable machinability and
  low frictional behavior. Electronic band structures and density
  of states indicate semiconducting properties, with HSE06 band
  gaps of 1.589 eV for PSiO3, 1.885 eV for AsSiO3, 2.791 eV
  for SbSiO3, and 3.206 eV for BiSiO3. Optical analyses show
  strong absorption and conductivity in the ultraviolet range and
  moderate absorption and conductivity in the visible range.
  Among the studied compounds, BiSiO3 shows promising thermal
  barrier coating potential due to its distinctly low Debye
  temperature and high melting point. These lead-free ASiO3
  compounds emerge as potential materials for photovoltaic,
  optoelectronic, and high-temperature applications.

summary: >-
  A first-principles DFT investigation of the structural, mechanical,
  electronic, optical, and thermal properties of cubic ASiO3
  (A = P, As, Sb, Bi) perovskites, revealing their semiconducting
  nature and potential for photovoltaic, optoelectronic, and
  high-temperature applications.
