abstract: >-
  This study presents a systematic first-principles investigation of the
  structural, electronic, elastic, thermodynamic and optical properties of
  orthorhombic XB2Mo (X = Sc, Zr, Nb) compounds using density functional
  theory (DFT) within the GGA-PBE and GGA-PBEsol exchange-correlation
  functionals. The optimized ground-state lattice parameters reveal stable
  orthorhombic structure with systematically varying unit cell volumes, in
  good agreement with available theoretical data. Band structure and DOS
  analyses confirm the metallic nature of the three compounds with the
  Fermi level dominated by transition metal d-orbital contribution from
  both X (Sc, Zr, Nb) and Mo atoms, indicating strong d-d hybridization.
  The calculated elastic constants satisfy the Born-Huang mechanical
  stability criteria, confirming mechanical stability at ambient
  conditions. All compounds are identified as brittle by both Pugh's ratio
  and Poisson's ratio criteria, while ZrB2Mo displays the highest elastic
  anisotropy with a universal anisotropy index of 6.15. Vickers hardness
  shows the order ScB2Mo > ZrB2Mo > NbB2Mo, while Tian's model yields
  nearly identical results for all three. Each compound surpasses the
  20 GPa threshold, confirming their classification as hard materials. The
  minimum thermal conductivity of XB2Mo diborides, estimated by Clarke's
  model, falls in the range of 1.49-1.63 W/m·K, underscoring their
  suitability as promising thermal barrier coating materials. NbB2Mo
  exhibits the highest bulk and Young's moduli, reflecting superior
  stiffness, while ScB2Mo shows highest Vickers hardness and Debye
  temperature making it most suitable for heat dissipation and wear
  resistance applications. Phonon dispersion calculations reveal no
  imaginary frequencies confirming dynamical stability of all compounds.
  The temperature-dependent heat capacity follows the Debye model with
  saturation near the Dulong-Petit limit at high temperatures. Optical
  analysis shows metallic reflectance at low energies, strong UV
  absorption/conductivity with NbB2Mo highest, and extinction peaks in the
  visible for ScB2Mo and infrared for ZrB2Mo/NbB2Mo, highlighting distinct
  optoelectronic roles. Collectively, these results establish XB2Mo
  (X = Sc, Zr, Nb) as a versatile and tunable family of hard metallic
  borides with strong promise for applications in UV optoelectronics,
  wear-resistant hard coatings, thermal barrier systems, and
  high-temperature structural components.
